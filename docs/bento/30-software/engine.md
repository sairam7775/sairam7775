# The engine: recommender and scheduler

The part that would be a fine-tuned model in a worse product. It's a **scoring function and a scheduler**: classical, explainable and tested against fixtures. Pure functions in `bento/src/lib/engine/`, with no framework, network or model, called from server code only.

## Scoring a place for this traveller

The reason shown with a stop is assembled from the terms that actually moved its score (`buildReason`): **the two strongest positive terms and, if large enough, one negative**. Some terms, such as confidence and pace strain, never become a clause. So the reason is true by construction, but it's a selection, not every term.

| Term | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| interest match | + 2.5 | Overlap between the place's tags and the traveller's interests (0–1) |
| signature | + 1.5 | How significant it is for a first-timer, set by hand (0–1) |
| confidence | + 1.0 | Verified 1.0, draft 0.4; stub places are excluded |
| season fit | + 0.8 | Whether a place's season (cherry blossom, autumn colour) is typically at its best in that city on that date. 1 inside the window, 0.3 outside it, 0.5 for a place with no season tags |
| discovery fit | + 0.5 | How well-known or off the beaten track, against the traveller's preference |
| redundancy | − 2.0 | The nth place of the same category; decays as 1/n |
| crowds | 1.2 | Crowds against the traveller's crowd tolerance |
| energy | 1.0 | Effort against the traveller's energy |
| skip if (mobility) | − 1.5 | The place's "skip if" against the traveller's constraints |
| budget strain | − 1.0 | Cost against the traveller's budget |
| pace strain | − 0.7 | Duration against what's left of the day |
| travel | − 1.0 per half hour | Extra travel to reach it |

The weights are the values in `score.ts`; the descriptions are summaries.

The weights are configuration (`bento/src/lib/engine/score.ts`), not constants; they're the first thing to tune against the evaluation set.

## Building a day (`planDay`)

1. **Budget:** the pace sets the active minutes: relaxed 360, standard 480, packed 600.
2. **Filter:** drop on hard rules, with no scoring: closed that weekday, on a date it closes every year (`closedYearly`, such as the year-end break; `closures.ts`: "closed 28 Dec – 1 Jan every year") or on a one-off date, booking required and not held, stub coverage, unverified, or a category the traveller excluded ("no beaches" means never). **The one exception is a stop the traveller pinned:** it stays even if it fails a rule, with a warning, and validation never removes a pinned stop.
3. **Anchor:** seed the day with the best place that has a best time window (Fushimi Inari at 07:00 anchors the morning). An evening-only place never opens the day.
4. **Grow:** add the place that gains the most score for the least extra travel, until the time is spent or nothing helps. There is no count of stops per pace: the budget decides (a guard of 12 stops, which only a packed day of short visits reaches). Relaxed holds fewer stops because its visits are the full ones and its budget the shortest; packed holds more because its visits are the short ones. Each place is tried on the clock before it is taken, at its best spot, then the others:
   - it must not make a pinned stop late (after closing, or after the time it's pinned for): "left out to leave time to reach … before it closes";
   - it must not leave itself or a stop already chosen past closing ("closes at …; you'd arrive at …");
   - it must not keep the day waiting more than 20 minutes for it to open (the point at which the day warns "the day waits … for it"): waiting isn't counted in the budget, so a late opener would stretch the day, not fill it. Only when nothing fits without a wait, the best place that fits with a wait of up to two hours is taken (an early riser's 07:00 start before anything near opens at 09:00); a longer wait leaves it out ("opens at 10:00, and the day would wait … min for it"). Evening-only places keep their own rule (below).
   - it must not end after the day's end, unless it is an evening place, or has an evening window and is in it: a 09:00 start, the pace's time and an hour for lunch (later for a later start), never after 19:00, so relaxed 16:00, standard 18:00, packed 19:00 ("the day would run past 18:00"). An early riser starts early to beat the crowds, not to finish early; a travel day starts later by the move and has that much less time, so it ends at the same hour Evening places and pins aren't held to it, so a day may still end after 19:30 with an evening stop (Tsukishima from 17:30, a night view) or a pin the traveller timed late;
   - it must be on the day's way: between two stops, not a detour of over 1.5 km off the straight line between them (Kyoto Tower between Yasaka Shrine and Gion); at the day's end, not back to an area the day already left while away from where it is now (Kappabashi after Kabuki-za, beside Senso-ji where the day began); at its start, the same the other way round, and not across town (over 1.5 km) from a pin the day opens with (Nijo Castle before Kiyomizu-dera). Distances are straight lines between places' positions; a place without one isn't checked. Nor where it would itself be off the way as validation counts it (below): at the day's start or end the rule above looks one way only, so Arashiyama, then the Fushimi sake district, then the Philosopher's Path each passed. Out of the way for now is retried as the day fills, and otherwise said: "out of the way of the rest of the day".

   An evening-only place (see "Rules it keeps") goes only after the day's other stops, at the cheapest such spot the day reaches within an hour of its evening by itself; if none does, and growing stops for want of anything else worth adding, it is left out with "best after 17:30, and this day ends before then" (a day that stops at the guard of 12, or at its share of a long stay, leaves it out without a reason, like any other place it leaves out). A place whose best windows are all evening but isn't evening-only (Hozenji) may go by day or after an evening stop. Lunch stays: an hour in the first gap between 11:30 and 14:00, outside the budget. A refill after a drop looks again at what an earlier pass left out for time. **A long stay shares its city:** `planTrip` and `plan_days` tell each day how many days are still to plan in that city (`cityDaysLeft`), and the day takes no more than its share of the places left (the places left times this day's part, divided by the days left, rounded down, never fewer than 4), so five packed days in Kyoto don't spend its places on the first three and leave the last nearly empty. A travel day counts for the part of a day its move leaves (`dayShare`: 360 of 480 minutes is three quarters). "Re-plan this day" (`replanDay`) counts this day and the later days of that city not planned yet: a planned day keeps its own places.
5. **Order:** reorder to cut total travel time, but never into an order that reaches a pinned stop after closing, or that puts a daytime stop after an evening one (neither move does). An evening stop waits for its evening in the search, so the stops after it are timed from there, and one the planner chose that would wait more than an hour costs as much as a closed stop. A timed pin is slotted with the same wait, so a pin at 18:00 goes before an evening stop that would still be running then. Two moves, repeated until neither helps: reverse a run of the planner's own stops (2-opt), and move a run of them, either way round, anywhere else in the day, past pins too (or-opt). Pins keep their order and the anchor keeps the front; only the stops the planner chose move. Before the second move a chosen stop could never cross a pin, so a day went Osaka Museum of History → Umeda → back to the castle beside the museum. The search weighs travel, 30 minutes for a busy place missing its quiet window, and a whole day (24 hours) for a stop finished after it closes, so no reordering trades a stop's hours for a shorter route. Nor into one that leaves a stop growing fitted past closing or the day's end, travels more than the day has time for (the quiet-window charge can trade travel for a quiet hour), keeps a chosen stop waiting over 20 minutes to open that wasn't before (Nishiki Market moved first for its quiet morning kept a 09:00 day waiting until 10:00), unless the order takes a return away, and then never over two hours (Tōshō-gū at 07:00, then Ekie, which opens at 11:00), or brings a stop back to an area the day left that wasn't before (counted across pins too). A timed pin is placed by the same clock `time` keeps, lunch and opening times included.
6. **Time:** set clock times from opening hours, train legs and meal slots, carrying slack forward. An evening stop starts no earlier than its evening (or its opening, if later); lunch goes before the wait, never after the evening. A wait of over two hours before a pinned evening stop gets a note: "Free time until 17:30, when Dotonbori is at its best".
7. **Validate:** re-check every hard rule on the finished day; on a failure, drop the offender and refill. A day that leaves an area and comes back to it loses the stop it came back for, but only if that really takes the return away: "left out so the day doesn't cross town and come back to …". A return is a stop within 600 m of an earlier one (or within a quarter of the way round, across a city: Kappabashi, 700 m from Senso-ji), reached by a detour of over 1.5 km in a straight line through the stops between. Not when a pin lies between them (the pins made the day cross town, and dropping the stops between would only strip it), nor a return to an evening stop in its evening. Growing and ordering keep to the day's way, so this is a last check. A chosen stop that takes the day more than 3.5 km further than the straight line between the stops either side, or a run of two more than 7 km (`DETOUR_DROP_KM`, `DETOUR_RUN_MAX`, `offWayOf`) goes too, the worst first, "out of the way of the rest of the day": a stop that opened the day across town could be moved between two others by ordering (Shinjuku Gyoen > Tsukiji Hongan-ji > Shibuya while the day waited for Shibuya's evening). Each such drop earns the day another pass to refill, up to six; a day of four stops or fewer keeps them (a long stay's last day is what is left of the city, and was stripped to two; `KNOWN_LOOPS` lists the four relaxed Tokyo days this keeps). A wait it leaves before an evening pin is free time, said in the day box (`dayStats.freeTime`). "Some places could not be fitted to their opening hours" is said only when the last drop was for opening hours. A pinned stop is never dropped: when one would still be reached after it closes, the nearest unpinned stop between it and the pin before it goes instead ("left out to leave time to reach … before it closes"), one per pass, and only if that really brings the pin earlier. It looks at each late pin in turn. A pin that is late whatever is dropped (its start is fixed by its own time, its opening time or the pin before it, or its visit is longer than its hours) keeps the stops around it and stays late.

It's greedy insertion with local search: not optimal, and it doesn't need to be. It needs to be right, fast and explainable. **Speed:** every ready-made trip at each pace and both start times (396 days) plans in about 16 ms a day on a desk machine, against about 8 before days grew to their budget: a fuller day prices more train legs and times more trial orders, and most of it is train routing. Clock strings are parsed once (`parseClock`, `parseWindow`; the caches hold at most 1,024 strings each and start again when full). `fill-shape.test.ts` fails above 60 ms a day.

### Travel days

A travel day is the day a traveller moves from one stay to the next: the arrival date of every stay after the first (`travelDayOrigins`, `bento/src/lib/engine/travel-day.ts`). That day's plan starts after the move, not at the usual hour, and has that much less time, so it ends when any day would and holds fewer stops.

- **The move** = `MOVE_ALLOWANCE_MIN` (60: checking out, getting to the departure station with bags, and from the arrival station to the new hotel to leave them) + the train between the two cities' hubs, as the route's legs count it (`cityTrainMinutes`). Packing comes before the usual start and checking in after the day, which is why it is less than the two hours `CHANGE_COST_MIN` charges a city change in the route verdict. The 60 is a planning allowance, not a measured figure.
- **No known train:** the allowance + `UNKNOWN_TRAIN_MIN` (150, longer than Tokyo to Kyoto, the longest single move a ready-made trip makes, 134 min hub to hub), and the day says so in a warning.
- The hubs are where a stay starts ([transit](transit.md), "Where a trip lands"): Osaka's is Osaka Station, so Osaka → Kyoto is the 29-minute Special Rapid, not the 13-minute Nozomi from Shin-Osaka, and Osaka → Hiroshima is 99 minutes (the JR Kyoto Line to Shin-Osaka, a 10-minute change, the Shinkansen).
- Examples, early riser (07:00): Osaka → Kyoto starts at 08:29 (60 + 29), Kyoto → Hiroshima at 09:40 (60 + 100); at 09:00, Tokyo → Kyoto starts at 12:14.
- A day spent in a zero-night city on the way (Himeji, between Kyoto and Hiroshima), or a day trip planned on the arrival date, starts after the move from the last stay to it. The travel-day card reads it the same way, date by date (`tripTravelDays`): from the stay slept in to the city saved for that date, then on to the new stay after the day when that city isn't it; with no zero-night city saved on the date, straight to the new stay. A route card (the proposal a ready-made start saves, `suggest_route`) takes a zero-night city as on the way only when the trip's `dayTrips` put it on the move day (`stopKinds` in `route.ts`); only a route with no days at all (a chat proposal) is guessed from the trains. Before this, all-nine-14's day 6 card went Tokyo → Yokohama → Kyoto because Yokohama, the day before, was "on the way". A day saved for another stay of the route on that date (planned before the route changed) is not moved: it is measured as a whole day. A day trip there and back from where the traveller sleeps is not a move, and neither is the trip's first day: arriving in Japan is a separate question, not planned yet.
- Pins on a travel day are kept as on any day. `planTrip` takes it from `TripDay.movingFrom` (set by `readyMadeToPlan`) with `trainMinutes`; `plan_days`, `replan_day` (and so "Re-plan this day") and the day box all work it out from the route with `travelDayMoves`, so they agree.
- A start given as a clock time (`dayStartMin`) is taken as it is, and the move still comes out of the day, so a re-planned travel day is no longer than the planned one. A **kept** start (a re-plan or an edit keeping its first stop) is never before the usual start plus the move (`travelDayStart`): a day saved before travel days allowed for the move starts after it on its next re-plan or edit. Nothing records whether a saved start was named, so an early start the traveller once named for an early train is lifted too, and the day says so: "This day now starts about 10:30, after you could arrive from Osaka. Say a start time to change it" (a re-plan's warning; after an edit, the page, from `note=start-lifted` in the address, which carries no words). Times about the move are said to five minutes. A start the traveller **names** (`start_at`) is taken as it is; if it is before they could arrive, the day says so ("This day starts at 08:00, before you could arrive…").
- A pin timed before the move is kept, reached when the day can, with the usual "pinned for … but the day only reaches it by …" warning.

`planDay` returns timed stops with a reason each, meals, budget and slack, the day's cost, and **everything left out, with the term that removed it**. `planTrip` runs the days in order, so no place is used twice, and keeps a place pinned to one day off every other day, so an earlier day can't take it first. Bento Man's `plan_days` doesn't do this yet (it plans day by day from saved pins; changing it changes a tool result and needs a live eval), so starting a ready-made trip will plan through `planTrip`.

## Rules it keeps

- The anchor stays first through growing and ordering; timed pins are placed where the clock reaches them.
- A busy place that would miss its quiet window costs 30 minutes in ordering.
- **Evening-only places are planned in the evening** (`eveningFrom`, `schedule.ts`). Evening-only is an explicit flag, never inferred from a best window: `eveningOnly` in the seed (`places.evening_only`, migration 0033), set only where the place's own notes say it is dead or closed by day. Seven are: Pontocho ("Dead by day"), teamLab Botanical Garden (light art after dark, from 19:45), Golden Gai (bars from 19:00, "quiet before nine"), the Hiroshima kagura (a Wednesday evening performance from 19:00), Nagarekawa ("quiet until eight") and Mount Ogonzan (the city's night view, "otherwise nobody"), and Omoide Yokocho (0035: yakitori counters that wake up after work; it was planned at 15:15). A place that opens at 17:00 or later (Tsukishima) counts too, by its hours. Its evening starts at its evening best window, or 17:00, or its opening if later. It is never planned before then: it goes after the day's other stops, or is left out. One the planner chose waits an hour at most for its evening; if ordering or a drop leaves it waiting longer, validation drops it with the same reason.
- **An evening best window alone is a soft preference** (Dotonbori, Gion, Hozenji, Umeda Sky, Abeno Harukas, Tokyo Tower, Skytree, Ameyoko, Shibuya Crossing: "after 17:30"): such a place may be planned by day, with the clause "after 17:30, when it empties out" when it falls in its window. Except as a pin: an untimed pin whose best windows are all evening (`eveningWindow`) is timed into its window, after the day's other pins, however long the day waits for it, so "Dotonbori on the first evening" holds. If its visit can't fit between the window and closing, it is timed like any pin and the day warns ("… is at its best after …, but it closes at …, too soon to visit then — planned by day because you pinned it"). A pin with a time of its own keeps that time. A place with a dawn and a dusk window (Fushimi Inari: "before 08:00", "after 17:30") isn't an evening place, and afternoon windows ("after 14:00", "after 16:00") stay soft.
- **"Unavoidable" only when it is.** A busy place (crowd 0.8 or more) with a "before" window, planned outside every window it has, says "busy at this hour. It's quieter before 08:00", naming every window ("before 08:00 or after 17:30" for Fushimi Inari). It says "busy at this hour, and unavoidable today" only when no start on that day reaches any window: the day's start is fixed (a travel day, or a start the traveller named: `startNamed`, set by `replan_day`'s `start_at`, not by the start a re-plan keeps from the day) and after the "before" window, or the place opens after it, and any "after" window closes too soon to visit. A 09:00 start isn't fixed: an early start would have reached the window. The planner doesn't reorder a pin to reach its quiet window (pins keep their order).
- Scores shown on stops are recomputed against the final order, so "your third temple today" is the third one shown.
- Hard rules live in `filter.ts` and never become penalties.
- **A hike ends in daylight** (`bento/src/lib/engine/daylight.ts`): a place of category `hike` with a position closes an hour before that day's sunset (`HIKE_DAYLIGHT_MARGIN_MIN`, an assumption: under trees the light goes first), or at its own closing if that is earlier. Sunset is NOAA's low-precision formula, good to a few minutes. Every hours check uses it; a hike left out says it would end in the dark, and a pinned one the day reaches too late stays, with that warning. Why: the Kamakura trail was planned 14:45–16:15 in mid-December, about sunset. Its "before 10:00" is a soft crowd window, so nothing stopped it.
- **Many shops shut is not closed** (`manyClosedWeekdays`, 0035): on those weekdays the planner doesn't choose the place (checked last of the hard rules, so a real closing is what a pin is warned of) ("many of its shops close on Wednesdays"); a pin stays with its own warning, not "kept because you pinned it", and a ready-made start is not refused (`closedMustSees` reads closing days only). Tsukiji Outer Market: Sundays and Wednesdays.
- **Booking and set times are said on the stop.** A place whose booking is recommended gets "book about N days ahead" in its reason (its booking lead, never a ticket release date), and a performance "shows start at set times". The vault raises a recommended booking only inside its lead time, so a day planned weeks ahead said nothing.
- Untimed pins keep the order they were given, except that an evening pin (one timed into its evening) goes after the others; the planner fills around them but doesn't reorder them. Its own stops may go before, between or after them, wherever the day is shortest, but never after an evening stop.
- Travel between two stops (`travelFromGraph`, `bento/src/lib/engine/travel.ts`) is the train route between their stations, with each end's walk, or a walk straight across when that is quicker. A straight walk is **estimated from the two places' positions, not measured**: the straight line × 1.3 (`WALK_DETOUR`) at 80 m a minute (`WALK_M_PER_MIN`), at least 3 minutes. All three figures, and the caps below, are assumptions chosen to err long. 80 m a minute is the pace Japanese property listings quote "minutes on foot" at, from memory and not checked against the listing rules; the 1.3 is a planning allowance for streets. The traveller reads it as an estimate: "Walk · about 14 min" (`copy.plan.transit.walkStraight`); a walk via the station keeps "Walk · 14 min". Neither repeats its minutes after it on the day view (`arriveMinutesSuffix`, `bento/src/lib/trips/arrive-line.ts`); a train journey's door-to-door total still follows its words.
  - In place of a train, or of the no-route estimate, only a straight walk of 20 minutes or less (`DIRECT_WALK_MAX_MIN`, an assumption: past that the straight line says too little about the streets). Two places by the same station were a walk already, `(walk + walk) × 0.8` via the station, and take the shorter of that and straight across, however long.
  - A straight line knows nothing of hills or moats (review found lines from Zeniarai Benten to Kotoku-in and over Kuzuharaoka, and from Osaka Castle's keep to the Museum of History). So a hike is never walked to or from in a straight line; a castle is walked to and from with a street allowance of 1.8 instead of 1.3 (`CASTLE_DETOUR`, an assumption: the way out goes round a corner of the moat to a gate, √2 ≈ 1.4 times the straight line, and the streets beyond add the usual 1.3; not measured on any castle), so Osaka Castle to the Museum of History is a walk of about 17 minutes, not 39 by train; and a steep place (`physicalDemand` 0.5 and over) that has a station never has one in place of a train. A steep place with no station does walk straight (same factor and caps): its only other time is the flat 45-minute guess, and the slope is there whichever way you arrive; before, Otokoyama in Himeji cost 45 minutes from anywhere, and a packed day went from the castle out to Mount Shosha and back to Otokoyama beside it. Beside another place by its own station a steep place keeps the shorter walk: both are a walk from that station anyway, and the slope is on either one (Kiyomizu-dera down through Ninenzaka).
  - A traveller with a hard mobility need (`HARD_MOBILITY`: wheelchair, limited mobility, step-free) gets straight walks of 5 minutes at most (`MOBILITY_WALK_MAX_MIN`, about 300 m, where a step or slope the positions can't show is least likely); past that their day goes by the stations as before. Every caller passes the traveller's mobility.
  - Why: without straight walks the graph went through Tanimachi 4-chome and Osakajokoen, so the museum across the road from the castle park looked half an hour away; within one station every order looked about the same, so Arashiyama went Okochi Sanso → down to Togetsukyo → back up the bamboo path.
- It never invents a train time. Walks straight across are estimated from positions, as above, and say so. A place with no station costs a flat 45 minutes and a warning, unless it is a short straight walk from the stop before or after (it has a position, as every seed place does): then that leg is the walk.
- A closure that comes every year is a month-day range, `"MM-DD..MM-DD"` or one `"MM-DD"`; an end before the start wraps the year (`"12-28..01-01"` is 28 Dec to 1 Jan). The same check (`closedYearlyOn`) leaves a place out of a day, warns about a pinned one ("Osaka Castle is pinned but closed 28 Dec – 1 Jan every year — kept because you pinned it"), and refuses a date change that would move a planned stop into it (`shift.ts`). Bento Man's `get_place` adds `closed_every_year` ("28 Dec – 1 Jan") next to the hours, only for places that have one.
- Only the returned day warns: its timing ("opens at …", "pinned for … but the day only reaches it by …") and its own legs ("has no station in Bento's train data", "no route between …"), each said once. The trial days and the candidates growing and ordering look at never add warnings. A no-station stop alone in its day has no leg to estimate, so it doesn't warn. `ready-made.test.ts` checks that no warning on any ready-made day names a place the day doesn't hold.
- **Seasons are typical windows, not months** (`bento/src/lib/engine/seasons.ts`). One table holds each city's typical best-viewing dates per season, as month-day ranges, with an area override where a city spans very different heights (Okunikko's lakes and falls colour weeks before Nikko's shrines). Each window comes from one named spot's "typical best viewing" (例年の見頃) on Weathernews (Nara's cherry blossom: tenki.jp, for its Somei-Yoshino), read November 2026, and the source sits beside it in the table. No margin is added either side, because no source gives one. Only a date inside the window counts as in season. Then the reason says "usually at its best around now", because the peak moves by a week or more from year to year and Bento makes no forecast. Outside the window a season tag earns no boost and no claim. A city with no windows (a new city, or a season the table doesn't list for it) never counts as in season. `bento/src/lib/engine/__tests__/seasons.test.ts` covers it, including 31 December and 8 March in Kyoto.
- **How full a day is** (`bento/src/lib/engine/__tests__/fill.test.ts`): an unpinned Kyoto or Osaka day uses at least 85% of its budget standard and 80% relaxed (less than one stop's worth left over: relaxed visits are the full ones, 45–180 min, so a gap of under an hour often holds none), and at least 68% packed (what standard must reach), never more than all of it. Packed is lower because its short visits reach the guard of 12 stops, most of these cities' sights close between 16:00 and 17:30, a chosen daytime stop ends by 19:00, and the seeds hold few evening places: the day stops rather than make one up or go out of its way. Over the ready-made trips' full days (a Wednesday start, both start times), the median day uses 94% relaxed, 95% standard and 77% packed; before, a fixed count of stops per pace (4, 5, 7) held them to 94%, 74% and 49%.
- **Known gaps:** opening windows are soft except for the anchor, evening-only places and evening pins; a day edited by hand (`retime.ts`) keeps the traveller's order and doesn't hold an evening place to its window, and neither it nor a date change (`shift.ts`) applies a hike's daylight or "many shops close"; season tags are optional and neutral when absent; season windows are per city (or area), not per place, and they don't follow a given year's forecast.

## Adding a place by hand (`fitPlace`)

"Add a place" on the day box ([day view](../10-product/day-view.md)) asks whether a place the traveller picked fits a day they already have. `fitPlace` (`bento/src/lib/engine/fit.ts`) chooses nothing about *what* goes in: the traveller did. It tries the place at every spot in the traveller's own order (an evening-only place only after the day's other stops), re-times each with `retimeDay`, and keeps the spot with the fewest problems, then the least time over budget, then the least added time; a tie goes to the later spot. It adds no rule of its own: each problem is the planner's, in the planner's words.

- **Hard rules:** `hardFilter` (closed that weekday, on that date or that date every year; a required ticket not held, by `match.ts`; an exclude; a hard mobility need; already on another day; many shops shut). Only its first reason is reported.
- **Conflicts:** `conflictsWith` either way with a stop already there (the Arashiyama trap).
- **The clock**, from `retimeDay`'s per-stop facts (`reachedMin`, `waitMin`, `pinShortMin`, `pastClosing`): past closing (a hike's closing is its daylight end, `daylightEndMin`); a wait over `OPEN_WAIT_MAX_MIN` for it to open; a daytime stop ending after the day's end (`dayEndFor`, the planner's rule, which `planDay` now calls too); another stop pushed past closing or past its pinned time; more time over the day's budget than before.
- **Evening-only** (`eveningFrom`): the day's other stops must reach it within `EVENING_WAIT_MAX` of its evening. It is added held at its evening start (a pin with that time), so re-timing never puts it in the afternoon.
- **Out of the way:** between two stops with positions, a detour over `DETOUR_DROP_KM` (3.5 km) off the straight line between them, the bar at which validation drops a chosen stop. The ends of the day have no such check; their travel counts in the budget.
- **Notes, not problems:** a leg with no route on record is the re-timer's 45-minute guess, and is said.

When it fits, the praise is only what's true: "it's on your way between A and B" (a detour within `RETURN_NEAR_M`), or "N min on foot from A" when the leg is a walk; the scheduler's window clauses when the day reaches its window ("starting 08:30, before it fills", "after 17:30, when it empties out", "planned for the evening, its best time"); then `scorePlace`'s positive clauses. At most three. The stop stores `buildReason`'s sentence, like a planned stop. Tests: `bento/src/lib/engine/__tests__/fit.test.ts`.

**A stop Bento doesn't know** (a free-text stop, 0036) can't be checked. `fitUnchecked` puts it at the end of the day for the length the traveller gave and reports only the clock's facts (over the budget, past the day's end); it is never called a fit. `retimeDay` takes it as `unchecked`: no hours, and the legs to and from it are the 45-minute fallback, stored as an `estimate` leg ("Travel time unknown — the day allows 45 min") so the day's sums count them. Before, the stop after an unknown one took the train from the stop before it. `planDay` never sees such a stop: a re-plan neither pins it nor fails on it, and its proposal shows it out.

## Ready-made trips

Implementation: partial (packages 1B and 1C of the ready-made trips plan: the trips, and starting one from "Plan a Japan trip"; no Bento Man tool reads them yet).

A ready-made trip is **saved input to the planner, never a hand-written itinerary**: its cities with nights, which day is a day trip, and up to three must-see places per day (the first trips use two at most). The engine builds the days for the traveller's own dates and pace, so a trip states no times, fares or durations.

- `bento/src/lib/trips/ready-made/`: `types.ts` (the shape, and `READY_MADE_CITIES`, the nine cities a trip may use), one file per trip, `index.ts` (the list). Titles, summaries and a reason per city are in `copy.readyMade.trips`.
- A trip of _n_ days has _n_ − 1 nights, the way a trip's end date works: the last day is spent in the last city. A zero-night city has its day named in `dayTrips`; a one-day trip is a single zero-night city.
- Must-sees are pins, listed in visiting order (the one that closes earliest first), because the planner doesn't reorder pins. It does put its own stops on either side of them, wherever the day is shortest. The first and last day of a longer trip carry one at most (arriving, leaving); a full day has two at most, one when they are long, so the planner still chooses something around them at every pace.
- `bento/src/lib/trips/__tests__/ready-made-order.test.ts` holds the days the 20 Nov read-through found going back and forth (Osaka in a day; the five-day trip's Dotonbori, Osaka Castle, Kyoto east and Arashiyama days; Kyoto's east side; the Arashiyama loops; the one-day Arashiyama trip starting by the Kamo river), each asserted on straight-line distances between the seeds' positions, not the engine's own minutes. Over every trip, pace and three starts, no day leaves a stop's area (600 m) and comes back by a detour of more than 3.5 km. In walking districts (Nara Park and Naramachi, Yokohama's bay, Shibuya at sunset) the shortest walk still loops back by up to about 3.4 km. Before the fix the worst was 10 km, and the average day's travel went from 59 to 45 minutes. A return to an evening stop doesn't count when the stop is in its evening (at or after its opening of 17:00 or later, its evening-only start, or its evening best window): the day ends there, and ordering can't fix it. The same sweep fails on any trip out of the day's way (above, 3.5 km). Over the ready-made trips (every pace, both crowd settings, four starts), that took the trips out over the bar (counted per stop and per pair) from 281 to 4 (the kept relaxed Tokyo days; two since 0035 keeps Tsukiji off Wednesdays, so `KNOWN_LOOPS` holds two), travel from 75.7 to 73.8 minutes a day and estimated ("no train data") legs from 139 to 96, for 2.5 fewer active minutes a day (399.9 to 397.4); days with over two hours free went from 148 to 157 of 1,584. `KNOWN_LOOPS` in the test lists day shapes known to loop back that are kept for now rather than fixed.
- A day spent in a zero-night city between two stays (Himeji, between Kyoto and Hiroshima, or Hiroshima and Osaka) is planned as an ordinary day in that city. The ride on to the next stay that evening and the luggage aren't planned, and the words don't promise "get off and get back on". The day page shows that ride below the day's stops, not beside the morning's train (`travelDayParts`).
- The trips through Hiroshima (`kansai-hiroshima-7`, `tokyo-to-miyajima-10`, `all-nine-14`) end in Osaka, for a flight home from Kansai Airport (the owner's call, 23 Nov 2026). The seven days loop from Kansai: Kyoto, Himeji on the way to Hiroshima, then Osaka with Nara.
- `readyMadeToPlan(trip, startDate)` turns one into the planner's inputs: the route in `propose_route`'s shape (with reasons), every date with its city and pins (`planTrip`'s days), and the day-trip dates in `plan_days`'s shape.
- `bento/src/lib/trips/__tests__/ready-made.test.ts` checks every trip against the seeds: ids exist and sit in that day's city, nights add up, `assessRoute` isn't overpacked and knows every train, no two must-sees on a day conflict, the first and last days are light, and `planTrip` fills every day, chooses at least one stop of its own on every full day (a relaxed travel day may hold only its must-sees: the move takes that time), never has to drop a stop to reach a must-see, keeps every must-see open and within its hours, never plans an evening place by day, and says "unavoidable" only on a travel day that starts after every quiet window. A must-see evening place is timed into the evening, so "Dotonbori on the first evening" (`osaka-kyoto-nara-5`) holds at every pace and start (`engine/__tests__/time-of-day.test.ts`). That is checked for a start on each weekday, a start in each season and two starts over the year-end break (30 Dec and 1 Jan), at each pace, with a 07:00 and a 09:00 start.
- What a season changes there is only the scoring (season tags). A place has one set of hours all year (the shortest, when they vary), so the test can't catch an hours change by season. Public holidays aren't in the place data, so they aren't checked either. Year-end closures are (`closedYearly`), for the places whose closure has been read from their own site.
- **A start that puts a must-see on a day it is closed is refused** (the owner's call, 21 Nov 2026), not planned with a warning. `closedMustSees(trip, startDate, places)` (`bento/src/lib/trips/ready-made/closed.ts`, pure) lists every must-see closed on its day for that start: that weekday (`closedWeekdays`), a one-off date (`closedDates`), or a date it closes every year (`closedYearlyOn`). The picker refuses such a start before writing anything. It names every closed must-see with its closure and its day, then the nearest start that keeps them all open (`nearestOpenStart`: up to 60 days either way, the earlier on a tie, never before the earliest day the form takes), or asks for another first day when there is none ("Osaka Castle is closed 28 Dec – 1 Jan every year, and this start puts it on Thu 31 Dec. The nearest first day that works is Fri 1 Jan.", `copy.readyMade.picker.errors`). `suggest_route` doesn't offer such a trip for a trip with a start date. With today's seeds, two must-sees refuse starts, both over the year-end break: Osaka Castle (its main tower, the paid part, closes 28 Dec – 1 Jan) and the Peace Memorial Museum (30–31 Dec). (Shukkeien Garden, 29–31 Dec, is no longer a must-see since the trips through Hiroshima end in Osaka.) The refused starts:
  - `osaka-day`: 28 Dec – 1 Jan (Osaka Castle).
  - `osaka-kyoto-nara-5`: 27–31 Dec (Osaka Castle).
  - `classic-7`: 23–27 Dec (Osaka Castle).
  - `kansai-hiroshima-7`: 22–28 Dec (Osaka Castle on 22–26 Dec, the Peace Memorial Museum on 27–28 Dec).
  - `tokyo-to-miyajima-10`: 19–25 Dec (Osaka Castle on 19–23 Dec, the Peace Memorial Museum on 24–25 Dec).
  - `all-nine-14`: 16–22 Dec (Osaka Castle on 16–20 Dec, the Peace Memorial Museum on 21–22 Dec).

  `ready-made.test.ts` pins this list, place by place, over every start from 11 Dec to 5 Jan (the seeds' yearly closures run from 25 Dec to 4 Jan, so that covers every start whose days can meet one), and plans every start in that window that isn't refused (at the standard pace only) as well as its weekday and season starts, failing on any closed must-see. Its weekday starts never meet a weekly closing day, so that part of the check never fires today and is there for completeness.
- **Before a trip is published,** every city it uses must be outline or deep on the live site: the planner refuses a city it doesn't know. The test treats every seed place as verified and can't see live tiers.
- **Starting one** (`bento/src/lib/trips/ready-made/start.ts`, called by `startReadyMadeTrip` in `bento/src/app/trips/actions.ts`): the traveller picks a trip, a start date (required: closing days need real dates; not before today in Japan, with a day's slack for travellers west of it) and a party size. The picker lists only trips whose every city, day trips included, is outline or deep in `city_coverage` (`availableReadyMade`); the others are left out, not shown as "coming". The action first counts the traveller's trips: at the database's cap of 100 it says so and reads nothing more (two starts at once can both pass the count; the database's cap then refuses the second, with the same message). It then checks coverage again, that every must-see is in `places_public` (`SupabaseCatalogue`, the same reads as `SupabaseStore`), and that none is closed on its day for this start (`closedMustSees`, above), before writing anything; the form keeps the traveller's answers on any refusal. It creates the trip as the traveller, so RLS, the trip cap and the write allowance apply. No rate limit (`take_rate_token`) applies to creating a trip, here or in the other trip forms: those buckets are for model calls. Then it runs `planTrip` with the traveller's saved preferences and the must-sees pinned, checks every pin is in the plan, and saves the route and the days as one proposal stored with a first chat message and applied at once through `applyProposal` (`apply_proposal`): the same path, and the same single database step, as an accepted chat proposal. If saving fails, including `apply_proposal` returning `stale_day` or an outcome the store can't read, the new trip is deleted (a failed delete is logged) and the traveller is told it didn't start. The route card's legs come from `assessRoute`, as in the chat: a day trip is there and back from where the traveller sleeps. Nothing calls the model. Tests: `bento/src/lib/trips/__tests__/ready-made-start.test.ts` (the memory store, plus `SupabaseStore`'s own append and apply over a stubbed client; not a real database).

## Why not ask a model

It runs in milliseconds and costs nothing. It's unit-testable, so "Arashiyama and Fushimi Inari in one afternoon" becomes a failing test rather than a complaint. It produces the same plan twice for the same input. And because every clause of the reason traces to a term, the traveller can disagree with the reasoning, not just the result.
